The Best Fitness Apps for Pilots and Flight Crew, Compared

By Chad Cooper, airline captainOct 19, 20266 min read

Key takeaways

  • The fitness apps built for pilots and crew in 2026 split four ways. Hotel workout apps (Flight Crew Fitness, CrewFit+, LayoverFit), an airport walking app (GateFit), paid human coaching (AeroFit), and a coaching community (Fit Pilot Accelerator). Pick by the problem you actually have, not by the feature list.
  • If your problem is training on a schedule that moves every week, you want an app that reads your trip and your sleep. If your problem is accountability, you want a person, and you will pay for one.
  • None of these apps has enough App Store ratings yet to show a score. Every one of them is new. Try the free tier before paying for anything, and judge it on a real four-day, not a day off.

The reason for writing it at all: a pilot who searches for a fitness app gets results built for people with a kitchen, a gym membership and a Monday that looks like last Monday. You have a four-day, three hotels, a 5 a.m. show and a dumbbell rack that stops at 30. A handful of apps are now built for that, and they solve different problems.

Training apps: the schedule is the hard part

Most of what makes training hard on the line is not the workout. It is the planning around duty days, commutes and layovers that change length every week. The training apps differ mainly in how much of that planning they take off you.

Flight Crew Fitness is the one I build. It imports your crew schedule, so it knows a 10-hour layover from a 26-hour one and a commute day from a reserve day. It reads Apple Health, which covers Apple Watch, Oura, Whoop and Garmin, and connects to Oura directly. A Go, Marginal or No-Go toggle, set from that data or a 15-second self-check, backs off or removes heavy lifting on the days your sleep says to. You pick the room: Commercial Gym, Hotel Gym, Hotel Room or bands. Hotel Room sessions skip jumping. There is a Runner objective for pilots who mainly run, and an opt-in crew feed where you can follow other crew, give kudos and comment. Free covers logging, basic trends, and hydration and pre-flight reminders. Pro adds the AI coach, food photo analysis, full trends history and the direct Oura connection. The web app works today; the iOS app is in App Store review.

CrewFit+ from Crew Health LLC takes the other approach: start from the hotel. Its listing says the strength library is tagged to more than 1,000 hotels worldwide, with over 2,000 workouts adapted to the equipment at the hotel you are in, and a Short Turn option that compresses a session to about 30 minutes. It also has CrewRun, GPS-mapped running routes near hotels with ratings and Crew Points for publishing routes, and CrewNutrition for calories, macros and water. If you want to know whether the gym has a cable machine before you take the elevator down, that is the strongest feature anyone in this category has. Check the price in the app: the description and the in-app purchase list disagree.

LayoverFit generates workouts with AI for people who live out of a bag, with bodyweight hotel room sessions or full gym sessions, rest timers, set tracking and streaks. Its standout line is that it works fully offline, so you can plan the session in cruise. The listing I found does not show a price for Pro.

Captain's note

Whatever you pick, judge it on a real trip. Any app looks good on a day off at home. The test is the second layover of a four-day, after a 5 a.m. show, when the hotel gym is a treadmill and a mirror. If the app still hands you something you will actually do in that room, keep it.

Walking apps: the connection you already have

GateFit does one thing and it is a good thing. Put in your flight and gate and it plans a walk between gates at major airports, with direct, scenic and loop routes, a boarding countdown that warns you when your goal will not fit, and a Terminal Passport that stamps each airport you walk. It saves the walk to Apple Health and it is free. It will not build you a strength session, and it does not try to. On a day with a 90-minute sit in Charlotte, it is the right tool.

Coaching: when the problem is accountability

Some pilots know exactly what to do and do not do it. That is not a knowledge problem, and an app full of workouts will not fix it. A person checking in every week sometimes will.

AeroFit runs on a coaching platform and is aimed at pilots, flight attendants and frequent travelers. The listing describes workouts built around your goals, equipment and travel schedule, calorie and macro targets, habit tracking for water, sleep and steps, progress photos, and in-app messaging with a coach. Access requires a paid coaching plan, listed at $199.99.

Fit Pilot Accelerator is not an app. It is a private community on Skool run by an active 737 captain who lists a NASM certification, at $10 a month with a free trial. It centers on a 90-day plan aimed at the aviation medical, with hotel and no-equipment workouts and airport food guidance. When I checked, the group was small, which can be a feature if you want the coach to know your name.

How to choose

Answer one question first: what actually stops you from training on a trip?

  • "I never know what I will have to work with." You want an app that adapts to the room. Flight Crew Fitness and CrewFit+ both do this, from different directions: one from your schedule and readiness, the other from a hotel equipment database.
  • "My sleep is wrecked and I do not know when to push." You want readiness data driving the session. Flight Crew Fitness is built around that.
  • "I sit through long connections." GateFit, today, for free.
  • "I start strong and quit by week three." Pay for a person. AeroFit or Fit Pilot Accelerator.
  • "I mainly run." CrewFit+ for routes near the hotel. Flight Crew Fitness for a weekly mileage plan that fits around your trips with one strength day a week placed away from the long run.

Two things to avoid. Do not stack three apps; you will log in none of them by the end of the month. And do not pay for a year before you have used the free tier on an actual four-day.

What none of them can do for you

No app gets you through the medical. The numbers your AME reads, seated blood pressure, weight and what follows from it, move slowly over years, and they move in response to the boring things: two or three strength sessions a week, a lunch bag that replaces terminal food, water on a schedule and protected sleep on the layover. The best app is the one that makes those four things easier on the trips you actually fly.

Questions pilots ask

What is the best fitness app for airline pilots?

It depends on the problem. For training around a trip schedule and your sleep, Flight Crew Fitness reads your crew schedule and readiness data and builds the session around the room you are in. For hotel-tagged workouts and GPS run routes near crew hotels, CrewFit+. For a human coach, AeroFit. For walking during a connection, GateFit. Most pilots are best served by one training app plus a habit they already keep.

Is there a free workout app for flight attendants?

Yes. Flight Crew Fitness has a free tier with workout logging, basic trends, hydration and pre-flight reminders. GateFit is free and turns a long connection into a walking route between gates. CrewFit+ and LayoverFit are free to download with paid tiers. The coaching options, AeroFit and Fit Pilot Accelerator, are paid. Check each listing for current prices, since several have changed this year.

Do pilot fitness apps work without hotel gym equipment?

The good ones do. Flight Crew Fitness has a Hotel Room environment with no jumping, so you do not wake the floor below, plus a resistance band option. LayoverFit lists bodyweight hotel room workouts and works fully offline. CrewFit+ adapts workouts to the equipment tagged for your hotel. A program that assumes a barbell is the wrong program for a four-day.

Should a pilot pay for an online fitness coach?

Pay for a coach when the problem is consistency, not knowledge. Most pilots know roughly what to do and struggle to do it on a moving schedule. A coach who checks in weekly fixes that for some people. AeroFit sells coaching inside an app, and Fit Pilot Accelerator is a captain-run group on Skool. If you already train on your own, a good app gets you most of the way for less.
