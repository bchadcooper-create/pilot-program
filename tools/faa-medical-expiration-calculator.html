How long an FAA medical lasts

Durations come from 14 CFR 61.23(d). They are counted in calendar months from the month of the exam, so every privilege ends on the last day of a month. Your age is the age you were on the exam date.

Privileges you are usingUnder 40 at the exam40 and over at the exam
ATP pilot in command (first class)12 calendar months6 calendar months
Part 121 second in command, commercial (second class)12 calendar months12 calendar months
Private, student, recreational, instructing (third class)60 calendar months24 calendar months

A certificate steps down rather than dying. A first class certificate is used at first class level until that period ends, then at second class level, then at third class level. A first officer holding first class keeps flying on its second class privileges after the first class ones lapse, unless the airline requires first class.

Which class your seat needs

  • Airline captain: first class, because you are using ATP pilot in command privileges.
  • Part 121 first officer: at least second class. First class at 60 and over, or in a flag or supplemental operation that needs three or more pilots. Many airlines ask for first class by policy.
  • Commercial flying for hire: at least second class.
  • Private, student, recreational, or instructing as pilot in command: third class. BasicMed is a separate route with its own rules and is not covered here.

When the ECG comes due

First class only. The FAA's examiner guide calls for an ECG at the first application after your 35th birthday, then on an annual basis after your 40th. If you are a 40-plus captain renewing every six months, that works out to an ECG at every other exam.

Captain's note

Book the exam early in the month it is due, not the last week. A blood pressure reading taken after a red-eye, or a form question that needs paperwork, is far easier to sort out with three weeks left on the certificate than with three days.

FAQ

How long is a first class medical good for?

For airline transport pilot privileges, a first class medical lasts until the end of the 12th month after the exam if you were under 40 on the exam date, and the end of the 6th month if you were 40 or older. After that it keeps working at second class level until the end of the 12th month, and at third class level until the end of the 60th month (under 40) or 24th month (40 and over).

Does a first officer need a first class medical?

Under 14 CFR 61.23 a Part 121 first officer needs at least a second class medical. First class is required at 60 and over, or in a flag or supplemental operation that needs three or more pilots. Many airlines require first class by company policy anyway, so check your own carrier's rule. A first officer holding a first class certificate keeps flying on its second class privileges after the first class ones lapse.

When do pilots need an ECG for a first class medical?

The FAA requires an ECG at the first application for a first class medical after your 35th birthday, then on an annual basis after your 40th birthday. Second and third class applicants do not need a routine ECG. Your AME does it in the office, so plan a few extra minutes at the appointment.

Is the medical expiration the exact date of my exam?

No. Medical certificate durations are counted in calendar months, so privileges run to the last day of the month, not the anniversary of the exam date. An exam on March 3 and an exam on March 28 expire on the same day. Your age is taken on the date of the exam, so turning 40 after the exam does not shorten that certificate.
