Common questions

Is Zoloft (sertraline) FAA approved for pilots?

sertraline (Zoloft): Special issuance path. One of the antidepressants the FAA allows, but only as a single agent through a HIMS AME after at least 3 months stable on it. The AME cannot issue in the office. Call your AME before you start it.

How long after taking Benadryl can a pilot fly?

diphenhydramine (Benadryl, ZzzQuil, Tylenol PM, Advil PM, Unisom SleepGels): Do not fly until the wait has passed. No-fly wait: 60 hours. Sedating antihistamine. The FAA wait is 60 hours after the last dose. It hides in many "PM" and "nighttime" products.

Can pilots take Ozempic or Wegovy?

semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus): Generally accepted. Accepted for diabetes (30-day observation when new) or for weight loss (one week, plus 48 hours after each dose change, no diabetes, A1c never 6.5 or more). Bring a recent clinical note.

How long after Ambien can a pilot fly?

zolpidem (Ambien, Ambien CR): Do not fly until the wait has passed. No-fly wait: 24 hours. Sleep aid. FAA wait: 24 hours. Occasional use for a time-zone problem is tolerated with the wait; routine use means the AME defers.

Can pilots take Viagra or Cialis?

sildenafil (Viagra): Generally accepted. No-fly wait: 8 hours. Accepted with an 8-hour wait before flying (FAA ED table). tadalafil (Cialis): Generally accepted. No-fly wait: 24 hours (as-needed dosing); none on daily low dose. As-needed use: 24-hour wait. Daily 2.5 or 5 mg: accepted with no wait after a 7-day trial. With an alpha blocker, no flying until it is clear you get no blood pressure drops.

What do DNI and DNF mean on the FAA medication list?

The FAA groups medications into Do Not Issue (DNI), which an Aviation Medical Examiner cannot certify while you take them, and Do Not Fly (DNF), which you may take but must not fly while they are active, usually five times the drug's longest half-life or a specific wait the FAA sets. Source: the FAA AME Guide, Do Not Issue / Do Not Fly page.

General FAA rule for anything sedating: wait five times the drug's longest half-life, or five times the dosing interval if the half-life is unknown. Any label that says "may cause drowsiness" means no flying while it is active. A new medication of any kind calls for a 48-hour ground trial after the first dose.
