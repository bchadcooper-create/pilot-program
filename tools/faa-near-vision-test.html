FAA vision standards by class

Each eye is tested separately, with or without corrective lenses. The numbers come from 14 CFR Part 67 and the FAA Guide for Aviation Medical Examiners.

VisionFirst and second classThird class
Distant20/20 or better20/40 or better
Near, at 16 inches20/40 or better20/40 or better
Intermediate, at 32 inches20/40 or better, age 50 and overNo requirement

Why this gets harder after 40

The lens inside the eye stiffens with age, so close focus drifts out. It is called presbyopia and it is the reason menus and approach plates end up at arm's length. It arrives gradually, which is why a lot of pilots first hear about it from the examiner.

Captain's note

If the 20/40 line took effort, book the optometrist before the AME. Walking in with new readers is a non-event. Missing the line in the chair turns a routine exam into a follow-up.

Glasses and the certificate

Needing lenses does not cost you the medical. If you need them to meet a standard, the certificate carries a limitation that you must wear corrective lenses, or have glasses for near vision available, while you fly.

How accurate a screen check is

A 20/20 letter covers 5 minutes of arc. At 16 inches that is 0.59 mm tall, and a 20/40 letter is twice that. Once the screen is sized against a card, this page draws the letters to those measurements. Lighting, glare and how exactly you hold 16 inches still move the result, so treat a miss as a reason to get checked and a pass as no obvious problem.

FAQ

What is the FAA near vision standard for pilots?

20/40 or better at 16 inches in each eye separately, with or without corrective lenses. It is the same for first, second and third class medical certificates.

Can I wear glasses or contacts for the FAA vision test?

Yes. The standards are met with or without correction. If you need lenses to meet a standard, the certificate carries a limitation saying you must wear or have them available while flying.

What is the FAA intermediate vision requirement?

From age 50, first and second class applicants must also read 20/40 or better at 32 inches in each eye separately, with or without correction. Third class has no intermediate requirement.

What is the FAA distant vision standard?

20/20 or better in each eye separately for first and second class, and 20/40 or better for third class, with or without corrective lenses.

Is a phone vision test accurate enough for the FAA medical?

No screen check replaces the examiner's chart. This one sizes the letters correctly once the screen is calibrated, so a miss is a good reason to see an optometrist before the exam, and a pass means no obvious problem. The AME's result is the one that counts.
